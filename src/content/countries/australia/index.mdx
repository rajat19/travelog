## Why Australia?

Australia feels enormous even when you only visit the southeast corner. Sydney and Melbourne are close enough to pair on one trip, but they offer completely different versions of the country: one all harbor light and ocean walks, the other all laneways, coffee, and cultural layers.

The best part is how much daily life happens outside. Even in the big cities, a good day can include a ferry ride, a swim, a market, and a long dinner without ever feeling overplanned.

<Highlight title="Australia First Pass">
- **Sydney**: Start with the harbor, then build the trip around beaches and coastal walks.
- **Melbourne**: Leave space for cafes, galleries, laneways, and a slower neighborhood rhythm.
- **Pace**: These cities reward unstructured mornings and sunset plans more than packed checklists.
</Highlight>

## The Rhythm

Sydney has a bright, cinematic confidence. Melbourne is more textured and understated. Together they make a strong first Australia itinerary because the contrast is immediate: ferries versus trams, beaches versus laneways, blue-sky landmarks versus brick-lined creative corners.

<Tip title="Build In Buffer">
Domestic flights between Sydney and Melbourne are frequent, but airports, transfers, and weather can still eat into a day. Keep the flight day light and use it for an easy dinner neighborhood rather than a major attraction.
</Tip>

## What Surprised Me

How strong the coffee culture is everywhere, not just in Melbourne. Flat whites become part of the daily structure, and breakfast feels like a real event rather than a rushed prelude to sightseeing.

## Planning Notes

Australia is not a budget destination, but many of the best experiences are public and low-cost: ferries, beaches, parks, coastal paths, galleries, and neighborhoods that are made for walking.

<Highlight title="Australia Essentials">
- **Transport**: Use Opal/contactless in Sydney and Myki in Melbourne.
- **Weather**: Pack layers. Melbourne can move through several moods in one day.
- **Sun**: UV is serious, even when the temperature feels comfortable.
- **Timing**: Shoulder seasons bring good walking weather and fewer peak-summer crowds.
</Highlight>
